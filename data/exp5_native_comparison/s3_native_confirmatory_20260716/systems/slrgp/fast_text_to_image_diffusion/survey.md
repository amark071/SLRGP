# Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

# Text-to-Image Diffusion Models: A Survey

## 1. Controllable and Personalized Text-to-Image Generation

Controllable and personalized text-to-image generation has emerged as a central research direction as diffusion models have matured beyond generic synthesis toward user-specific and spatially precise outputs. One prominent line of work focuses on personalizing generation to reflect individual aesthetic preferences. Gallego [arxiv_2209.12330] proposes aesthetic gradients, a method that guides a CLIP-conditioned diffusion model toward custom visual aesthetics derived from a user-supplied set of images. By steering the generative process through these gradients within Stable Diffusion, the approach enables qualitatively and quantitatively validated personalization without retraining the full model. Building on the broader goal of personalization, Zhang et al. [arxiv_2305.16225] identify a more granular challenge: while existing methods can invert objects or concepts into the textual conditioning space, representing and editing specific visual attributes such as material, style, and layout remains difficult. Their ProSpect framework addresses this through a prompt spectrum that disentangles attribute-aware representations, offering finer control than aesthetic-level personalization alone. Together, these two works illustrate a progression from coarse aesthetic steering toward structured attribute decomposition.

A complementary challenge concerns spatial controllability. Avrahami et al. [arxiv_2211.14305] observe that despite the impressive quality of text-to-image diffusion models, fine-grained control over the shapes and layout of individual regions remains elusive, particularly because prior approaches relied on fixed label sets. Their SpaText method introduces spatio-textual representations that decouple regional semantics from global prompts, enabling open-vocabulary spatial control. Separately, Zhong et al. [arxiv_2305.05189] address a different bottleneck—semantic understanding—by augmenting pre-trained diffusion models with large language models via a SUR-adapter, improving commonsense reasoning when input prompts are concise or narrative in nature. Collectively, these works highlight that controllable generation requires advances across aesthetic, attribute, spatial, and semantic dimensions simultaneously.

Iterative human feedback represents a further axis of personalization. FABRIC [arxiv_2307.10159] integrates preference signals directly into the diffusion process, allowing users to refine outputs through successive feedback rounds and positioning human preference as an active signal rather than a passive evaluation criterion. This approach complements concept-level personalization by operating at the level of perceptual judgement rather than semantic specification. Concept ablation techniques [arxiv_2303.13516] extend controllability in the opposite direction, enabling selective removal of specific styles or memorized identities from pre-trained models without full retraining—an important capability for responsible deployment. Together, these contributions trace a trajectory from coarse aesthetic steering through structured attribute decomposition and spatial grounding toward fine-grained, user-steerable, and ethically auditable generation.

## 2. Text Rendering and Typography in Diffusion Models

### 2.1 Early GAN-Based Foundations

Early GAN-based approaches to text-to-image synthesis established foundational techniques for conditioning image generation on natural language descriptions. SegAttnGAN [arxiv_2005.12444] incorporated segmentation attention to provide structural guidance during generator training, yielding improvements in both realism and quantitative performance over earlier state-of-the-art methods. XMC-GAN [arxiv_2101.04702] pursued semantic fidelity through cross-modal contrastive learning, maximising mutual information between image and text representations. These methods demonstrated increasing sophistication in aligning visual outputs with textual conditions, yet they struggled with fine-grained character-level rendering—a challenge that becomes particularly acute in scene text and typography generation.

Within the typography domain specifically, Matsuda et al. [arxiv_2103.10036] proposed Impressions2Font (Imp2Font), an extended conditional GAN framework that accepts an arbitrary number of impression words as conditioning signals to generate font images reflecting specific typographic impressions. This work established a foundational paradigm for attribute-driven font synthesis, showing that the perceptual and stylistic properties of typefaces could be meaningfully encoded through natural language descriptors. The TE141K benchmark [ss_481846] complemented this direction by cataloguing diverse artistic text effects to support data-driven text stylization research, while SynthTIGER [arxiv_2107.09313] provided a synthetic text image generator designed to produce diverse typographic training data for scene text recognition models.

### 2.2 Diffusion Model Conditioning and Spatial Control

Early text-to-image diffusion models established strong baselines for photorealistic synthesis by leveraging web-scale paired datasets, yet persistent challenges remained in achieving reliable text-image correspondence, particularly in domain-specific settings [arxiv_2308.08157]. Improving sample quality within these frameworks has been an active area of inquiry; refinements to the sampling strategy of Vector Quantized Diffusion models were shown to substantially reduce low-quality outputs and weak text-image alignment [arxiv_2205.16007]. Broader surveys of the field have catalogued these recurring issues alongside proposed remedies, situating individual contributions within a rapidly evolving landscape of models such as Imagen, DALL-E 2, and Stable Diffusion [arxiv_2308.13142].

A complementary line of research has focused on conditioning mechanisms that afford users greater spatial and semantic control. Text-driven stylization approaches such as DiffStyler exploit dual diffusion conditioning to transfer style according to textual descriptions, offering more intuitive control than image-to-image transfer paradigms [arxiv_2211.10682]. Localised editing methods like iEdit demonstrate that diffusion models can be conditioned on both a source image and an edit prompt to achieve targeted modifications, though controllability in the output space remains a noted limitation [arxiv_2305.05947]. The challenge of multi-prompt spatial guidance has also been addressed through differentiable optimal transport, enabling simultaneous conditioning on multiple textual cues to steer generation toward compositionally complex targets [arxiv_2204.08472]. Grounded generation frameworks such as GLIGEN further augment controllability by conditioning pre-trained diffusion models on spatial grounding inputs beyond text alone [arxiv_2301.07093].

Beyond spatial control, conditioning fidelity is complicated by the tendency of large diffusion models to memorise training data, raising concerns about replication of copyrighted content [arxiv_2305.20086]. Attention-based control strategies have been proposed to better align generated images with compositional textual descriptions [arxiv_2305.13921], and DenseDiffusion extends this direction to dense, region-specific captions through training-free attention modulation [arxiv_2308.12964]. An energy-based cross-attention framework proposed by Park et al. [arxiv_2306.09869] addresses semantic misalignment as a related failure mode, wherein generated images do not faithfully capture the intended content of text prompts.

### 2.3 Text-Driven Image Editing

InstructPix2Pix demonstrated that combining a large language model with a text-to-image model to synthesize paired training data allows a single network to follow free-form human editing instructions at inference time [arxiv_2211.09800]. More granular control is addressed by iterative multi-granular editing frameworks that support global, local, and intermediate spatial modifications across successive editing rounds [arxiv_2309.00613]. DreamInpainter extends this space further by introducing subject-driven inpainting that jointly conditions on both text prompts and exemplar images—a combination previously left unexplored and requiring careful balance between editability and fidelity to the reference subject [arxiv_2312.03771].

A notable limitation of early editing approaches was their restriction to low resolutions; multi-stage blended diffusion pipelines address this by leveraging pre-trained low-resolution models to perform edits at megapixel scale [arxiv_2210.12965]. Recognising that language can be ambiguous for specifying precise edits, visual instruction inversion methods allow users to convey intended transformations through visual prompts rather than text alone [arxiv_2307.14331]. Extending editing capabilities to the temporal domain, FateZero achieves zero-shot text-based video editing by fusing cross-frame attentions without per-prompt training, demonstrating that inversion-based editing principles transfer beyond static images [arxiv_2303.09535]. Collectively, these editing approaches reveal a progression from coarse instruction-following toward increasingly precise, multi-conditional control.

Evaluation of these systems poses its own challenges. X-IQE proposes an explainable quality assessment framework using visual large language models and hierarchical chain-of-thought reasoning to produce human-aligned textual judgements of generated images [arxiv_2305.10843], offering a more interpretable alternative to scalar metrics for benchmarking both generation and editing systems.

### 2.4 Font, Scene Text, and Markup Generation

The emergence of large-scale diffusion models introduced new capabilities for controllable image synthesis relevant to typography. Personalisation and attribute-aware generation methods such as ProSpect [arxiv_2305.16225] showed that decomposing the prompt space into attribute-specific components could improve control over visual properties including style and layout—properties directly relevant to typographic generation. Word-As-Image presents an automatic method for semantic typography, wherein letter forms are deformed to visually convey word meaning while preserving legibility [arxiv_2303.01818]. A distinct but related contribution is the markup-to-image diffusion approach of [arxiv_2210.05147], which frames the denoising process as sequential decision-making to render structured markup languages directly into images, demonstrating that diffusion models can serve as fully data-driven rendering engines for structured textual inputs.

TextDiffuser tackled the persistent failure of diffusion models to render coherent textual content within generated images by introducing a two-stage pipeline in which a Transformer first predicts keyword layouts before guiding the diffusion process [arxiv_2305.10855]. MagicFusion proposes Saliency-aware Noise Blending to ensemble multiple pretrained diffusion models, combining their respective strengths to boost generation performance [arxiv_2303.13126]. Conceptor investigates the internal representations of diffusion models by decomposing textual concepts to illuminate what these models actually learn [arxiv_2306.00966]. Together, these works illustrate a trajectory in which diffusion models are progressively extended beyond naturalistic image synthesis toward precise typographic and structural rendering.

## 3. Few-Shot and Single-Image Diffusion Training

### 3.1 Architectural Advances and Compositional Fidelity

Text-to-image diffusion models have demonstrated remarkable capacity for synthesizing high-quality images from natural language descriptions. Hierarchical approaches such as Swinv2-Imagen extend this capability by integrating advanced vision transformer backbones into the diffusion pipeline [arxiv_2210.09549], while systematic exploration of vision transformers as diffusion learners has further refined backbone design choices [arxiv_2212.13771]. Despite these architectural advances, a persistent challenge concerns compositional fidelity: models frequently exhibit attribute leakage, entity confusion, and missing objects when conditioned on complex prompts.

Large-scale diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art results on text-to-image tasks, but attribution-binding and compositional generation—particularly when multiple objects are involved—remain significant limitations [arxiv_2212.05032]. For complex scenes requiring coherent global structure alongside fine-grained object detail, feature pyramid diffusion approaches that perform multi-scale coarse-to-fine denoising offer a promising architectural direction [arxiv_2208.13753]. Retrieval-augmented diffusion models extend text-guided synthesis toward artistic image generation by incorporating external knowledge at inference time [arxiv_2207.13038], while KNN-Diffusion [arxiv_2204.02849] leverages efficient k-nearest-neighbor search to enable generation in low-data domains without full retraining.

Non-autoregressive alternatives have also been proposed to mitigate the high inference latency common to both autoregressive and diffusion paradigms, where hundreds of sequential denoising steps impose substantial computational costs [arxiv_2312.14988]. Within the space of few-shot and single-image adaptation, ShiftDDPMs propose shifting diffusion trajectories to better incorporate conditioning signals without retraining from scratch [arxiv_2302.02373], and self-conditioned diffusion models demonstrate that partitioning data appropriately during training can close much of the performance gap between unconditional and conditional generation [arxiv_2212.00362].

### 3.2 Image-to-Image Translation

SDEdit introduced a foundational approach using stochastic differential equations to balance faithfulness to user inputs against photorealism [arxiv_2108.01073], establishing a framework that subsequent methods have extended. Optimal transport-guided score-based diffusion models address the practically important setting of partially paired or unpaired data, enabling image translation when full supervision is unavailable [arxiv_2311.01226]. Text-driven stylization methods such as DiffStyler build on these foundations by using dual diffusion processes to transfer styles described textually rather than via reference images [arxiv_2211.10682], offering users more intuitive control than image-guided alternatives. Extending translation to the temporal domain, MeDM leverages pretrained image diffusion models for video-to-video translation by incorporating explicit optical flow as temporal correspondence guidance [arxiv_2308.10079], demonstrating that image-domain diffusion priors can be repurposed for sequential data with appropriate structural constraints.

### 3.3 Accelerating Diffusion Model Sampling

Text-to-image diffusion models have achieved remarkable generative quality, yet several interconnected challenges—ranging from training data constraints and architectural limitations to prohibitive inference costs—continue to motivate active research. Standard diffusion pipelines require hundreds of sequential forward passes to produce a single high-fidelity output, making inference prohibitively slow for many applications.

One foundational approach involves learning efficient noise schedules rather than relying on fixed, hand-crafted ones. Watson et al. [arxiv_2106.03802] demonstrated that the discrete-time step sequence used during sampling can be optimized directly, enabling high-quality generation with far fewer denoising steps. Building on this, Watson et al. [arxiv_2202.05830] introduced Differentiable Diffusion Sampler Search (DDSS), which differentiates through sample quality metrics to optimize fast samplers for any pre-trained diffusion model. Whereas [arxiv_2106.03802] focuses on selecting an optimal subset of timesteps, [arxiv_2202.05830] extends the optimization to sampler parameters more broadly, offering greater flexibility across model architectures. San-Roman et al. [arxiv_2104.02600] proposed a learning scheme that iteratively adjusts noise parameters for any desired number of denoising steps, alleviating expensive manual tuning and applicable post-hoc to existing models without retraining.

At the systems level, ReDi [arxiv_2302.02285] introduces a learning-free retrieval-based framework that shortens diffusion trajectories by retrieving precomputed intermediate states, while post-training quantization [arxiv_2305.10657] reduces model size and latency without retraining. Approximate caching [arxiv_2312.04429] reduces redundant computation across similar prompts in production serving environments. More radically, a non-autoregressive generation paradigm [arxiv_2312.14988] bypasses the sequential denoising bottleneck shared by both diffusion and autoregressive models, achieving substantially lower inference latency. Together, these works illustrate that acceleration efforts span model compression, sampling strategy, retrieval augmentation, and architectural redesign.

### 3.4 Non-Diffusion and Hybrid Generative Approaches

Early non-diffusion approaches to text-to-image generation relied predominantly on GANs, which faced fundamental challenges in bridging semantic and visual representations. Yuan and Peng identified two core obstacles—the heterogeneous gap between high-level textual concepts and pixel-level image content, and the homogeneous gap arising from intra-modal variation—and proposed symmetrical distillation networks to address both simultaneously [arxiv_1808.06801]. Ye, Liu, and Tan introduced recurrent affine transformation modules to enable continuous, context-aware fusion of text information across multiple synthesis stages [arxiv_2204.10482], while Gou et al.'s SegAttnGAN [arxiv_2005.12444] incorporated segmentation attention to provide structural guidance during generator training.

A recurring practical limitation across GAN-based pipelines concerns data availability. Menardi et al. addressed this constraint by leveraging machine-generated captions to expand training corpora, demonstrating that automatically produced textual annotations could substitute for costly human-authored descriptions without severely degrading synthesis quality [arxiv_1910.04056]. This strategy anticipates later concerns in diffusion-based paradigms about data curation and scale.

Within the diffusion literature, reinforcement learning has been explored as a fine-tuning mechanism: DPOK [arxiv_2305.16381] demonstrated that optimizing diffusion models against learned reward functions derived from human feedback yields more semantically faithful outputs than simpler rejection-sampling baselines. Concept ablation methods [arxiv_2303.13516] further extended controllability by enabling selective removal of copyrighted styles or memorized content from pre-trained models without full retraining, highlighting the growing importance of post-hoc model editing in responsible deployment.

## 4. Synthetic Image Data for Training and Evaluation

### 4.1 Accelerating and Optimizing Diffusion Sampling

The practical deployment of text-to-image diffusion models is constrained by the substantial computational cost of iterative sampling. Several lines of research have addressed this bottleneck through complementary strategies. As discussed in Section 3.3, Watson et al. [arxiv_2106.03802] and [arxiv_2202.05830] established that noise schedules and sampler parameters can be jointly optimized to reduce step counts without sacrificing quality, while San-Roman et al. [arxiv_2104.02600] demonstrated post-hoc noise parameter adjustment. Architecturally, [arxiv_2207.11192] questions whether standard noise-based diffusion is optimal, proposing a coarse-to-fine progressive deblurring process that better respects human perceptual sensitivity to low-frequency image content, while [arxiv_2303.05456] reframes denoising diffusion through the lens of image restoration, enabling alternative training objectives and improved synthesis quality. Complementarily, [arxiv_2308.16582] addresses resolution-induced composition failures in Stable Diffusion by developing a framework for generating high-definition images at arbitrary sizes without retraining on impractically large inputs.

### 4.2 Conditioning, Guidance, and Controlled Generation

Conditioning and controlled generation represent central concerns in the development of text-to-image diffusion models. A foundational challenge is that standard text conditioning frequently fails to faithfully reproduce all specified attributes, leading to attribute leakage, entity omission, and semantic misalignment. Several works address this through attention-level interventions: [arxiv_2305.13921] proposes an attention mask control strategy to enforce compositional fidelity, while the energy-based cross-attention framework of Park et al. [arxiv_2306.09869] addresses semantic misalignment through adaptive context control. DenseDiffusion [arxiv_2308.12964] extends attention modulation to dense, region-specific captions in a training-free manner.

Multi-modal and plug-and-play conditioning strategies extend control further. Retrieval-based conditioning [arxiv_2204.02849] and [arxiv_2207.13038] both augment generative models with nearest-neighbour retrieval, reducing the burden on parametric model capacity and enabling adaptation to data-scarce domains. Models trained exclusively on Creative Commons-licensed imagery [arxiv_2310.16825] address copyright concerns that plague models trained on uncurated web data—a problem also highlighted by concept ablation techniques [arxiv_2303.13516]. Personalization through aesthetic gradients [arxiv_2209.12330] and attribute-aware prompt decomposition [arxiv_2305.16225] offer lightweight adaptation without full retraining, while FABRIC [arxiv_2307.10159] positions iterative human feedback as an active conditioning signal.

### 4.3 Fine-Tuning and Adapting Pretrained Diffusion Models

Recent advances in text-to-image diffusion models have opened new avenues for adapting pretrained generative systems to specialized tasks. Gallego [arxiv_2209.12330] introduces aesthetic gradients as a lightweight personalization method that steers a CLIP-conditioned diffusion model toward user-defined aesthetic preferences derived from a small reference image set, demonstrating that meaningful stylistic adaptation can be achieved without full retraining. A retrieval-augmented alternative is explored by both Sheynin et al. [arxiv_2204.02849] and Rombach et al. [arxiv_2207.13038]: the former employs k-nearest-neighbor retrieval to enable training on small or unlabeled domain-specific datasets, while the latter integrates retrieval-augmented conditioning to guide artistic image synthesis. Whereas aesthetic gradients operate through gradient-based guidance at inference time, retrieval-based approaches embed external knowledge directly into the generative pipeline, representing a complementary axis of adaptation.

DPOK [arxiv_2305.16381] demonstrates that reinforcement learning from human feedback can further align pretrained models with user intent, while concept ablation [arxiv_2303.13516] enables selective post-hoc editing of model knowledge. Together, these works illustrate that pretrained diffusion models can be efficiently specialized—whether through aesthetic steering, retrieval augmentation, reinforcement learning, or domain-specific fine-tuning—while the utility of the resulting synthetic outputs for downstream training and evaluation continues to depend critically on how well generated images preserve semantic and spatial fidelity.

### 4.4 Synthetic Data from Diffusion Models for Downstream Vision Tasks

The use of synthetic imagery produced by text-to-image diffusion models as training and evaluation data for downstream vision tasks has attracted growing scholarly attention. A central question concerns whether synthetically generated images can substitute for real training data. The tendency of diffusion models to memorise training images [arxiv_2305.20086] raises questions about the independence and diversity of synthetically generated datasets used for model evaluation or augmentation, tempering enthusiasm for diffusion-generated training corpora.

Compositional control over synthetic data generation remains a persistent challenge. Structured diffusion guidance [arxiv_2212.05032] addresses attribution-binding failures in multi-object scenes, a concern directly relevant when synthetic data must reflect specific relational or categorical distributions required by downstream tasks. TextDiffuser [arxiv_2305.10855] addresses the accurate rendering of textual content within generated images, while improvements to vector quantized diffusion sampling [arxiv_2205.16007] offer gains relevant to large-scale compositional synthesis. Collectively, these contributions delineate both the promise and the current limitations of leveraging diffusion-generated synthetic data within broader vision pipelines.

## 5. Extending Diffusion Models to New Modalities and Tasks

### 5.1 Personalization and Multi-Concept Customization

Personalization of text-to-image diffusion models has emerged as a central research challenge, with efforts focused on enabling models to faithfully reproduce user-specified concepts while remaining computationally tractable. FABRIC [arxiv_2307.10159] incorporates iterative human feedback directly into the generative process, allowing users to refine outputs through successive feedback rounds. This approach complements concept-level personalization by operating at the level of perceptual judgement rather than semantic specification, and positions human preference as an active signal in personalization rather than a passive evaluation criterion.

Retrieval-augmented approaches [arxiv_2204.02849] offer a complementary strategy by reducing the burden on parametric model capacity, enabling adaptation to data-scarce domains without per-concept fine-tuning. Aesthetic gradients [arxiv_2209.12330] and attribute-aware prompt decomposition via ProSpect [arxiv_2305.16225] provide lightweight alternatives that operate through gradient-based guidance or prompt engineering respectively. DPOK [arxiv_2305.16381] extends personalization to the reinforcement learning setting, demonstrating that reward-guided fine-tuning yields more semantically faithful outputs than simpler baselines. Together, these works illustrate a spectrum of personalization strategies ranging from inference-time steering through lightweight fine-tuning to full reward-based optimization.

### 5.2 Compositional and Large-Scale Image Generation

Compositional and large-scale image generation represents one of the most actively investigated frontiers in text-to-image diffusion research, as standard models frequently struggle to faithfully render scenes involving multiple objects, precise spatial arrangements, or complex attribute bindings. A foundational difficulty is that direct text-to-image mappings tend to capture salient keywords while failing to learn the spatial distribution patterns of the described elements. Layout-bridging approaches introduce intermediate spatial representations that enforce cross-modality semantic consistency between input text and synthesized output, while attention refocusing strategies correct misaligned cross-attention and self-attention activations that underlie the model's inability to respect multi-object or spatially explicit prompts [arxiv_2305.13921].

SpaText [arxiv_2211.14305] addresses the spatial control problem by introducing spatio-textual representations that allow region-level layout guidance without relying on fixed label sets. GLIGEN [arxiv_2301.07093] augments controllability by conditioning pre-trained diffusion models on spatial grounding inputs beyond text alone. DenseDiffusion [arxiv_2308.12964] extends attention modulation to dense, region-specific captions in a training-free manner. Beyond spatial composition, accurate rendering of textual elements within generated images poses a separate challenge addressed by TextDiffuser [arxiv_2305.10855], which decomposes generation into a keyword layout stage followed by diffusion-based rendering. Improvements to vector quantized diffusion sampling [arxiv_2205.16007] offer further gains relevant to large-scale compositional synthesis. Collectively, these contributions illustrate a convergence toward architectures and training objectives that enforce finer-grained semantic fidelity, spatial grounding, and element-level accuracy.

Resolution presents an additional challenge for large-scale generation. [arxiv_2308.16582] addresses resolution-induced composition failures in Stable Diffusion by developing a framework for generating high-definition images at arbitrary sizes without retraining, while feature pyramid diffusion approaches [arxiv_2208.13753] perform multi-scale coarse-to-fine denoising to handle complex scenes requiring coherent global structure alongside fine-grained object detail.

### 5.3 Extending Diffusion Models to New Modalities

The extension of diffusion models beyond static image generation to richer modalities such as video, motion, and structured markup represents a significant frontier in generative modelling research. Text-to-video synthesis has emerged as a particularly active area, with recent work demonstrating that text prompts alone are often insufficient to govern precise spatial and temporal composition. FateZero [arxiv_2303.09535] achieves zero-shot text-based video editing by fusing cross-frame attentions without per-prompt training, demonstrating that inversion-based editing principles transfer beyond static images. MeDM [arxiv_2308.10079] leverages pretrained image diffusion models for video-to-video translation by incorporating explicit optical flow as temporal correspondence guidance, demonstrating that image-domain diffusion priors can be repurposed for sequential data with appropriate structural constraints.

In the domain of structured markup, the markup-to-image diffusion approach of [arxiv_2210.05147] frames the denoising process as sequential decision-making to render structured markup languages directly into images, demonstrating that diffusion models can serve as fully data-driven rendering engines for structured textual inputs. This unification agenda—extending diffusion models to handle temporal, structural, and multi-modal complexity—reflects a broader ambition to develop modality-agnostic diffusion architectures, positioning video and geometric data not as isolated domains but as instances of a common generative framework.

### 5.4 Interpreting and Analyzing Diffusion Model Internals

Efforts to interpret diffusion model internals have increasingly focused on the design space of internal representations. Conceptor [arxiv_2306.00966] investigates the internal representations of diffusion models by decomposing textual concepts to illuminate what these models actually learn, providing a principled basis for understanding how conditioning signals are encoded. MagicFusion [arxiv_2303.13126] proposes Saliency-aware Noise Blending to ensemble multiple pretrained diffusion models, combining their respective strengths—an approach that implicitly requires understanding which internal components contribute most to generation quality.

The energy-based cross-attention framework of Park et al. [arxiv_2306.09869] addresses semantic misalignment by intervening at the level of attention activations, offering mechanistic insight into why standard conditioning fails for complex prompts. Attention mask control strategies [arxiv_2305.13921] and DenseDiffusion [arxiv_2308.12964] similarly operate at the level of internal attention maps, reflecting a broader trend toward mechanistic interpretability as a basis for controlled generation. X-IQE [arxiv_2305.10843] complements these internal analyses with an external evaluation framework, using visual large language models and hierarchical chain-of-thought reasoning to produce human-aligned textual judgements of generated images—offering a more interpretable alternative to scalar metrics. Together, these contributions illustrate a maturing research agenda that moves from empirical generation toward principled interpretation of diffusion model internals.

### 5.5 Safety, Ethics, and Responsible Deployment

The responsible deployment of text-to-image diffusion models has emerged as a pressing concern alongside their rapid capability gains. A central issue is the tendency of these models to reproduce and amplify social biases: even when neutral prompts are used, models exhibit strong preferences for particular social groups, and explicitly adding ethical language to prompts yields only partial improvements, underscoring the depth at which bias is encoded in model weights.

Beyond demographic bias, the moral acceptability of generated content represents a distinct but related challenge. Post-generation filtering mechanisms that engage with commonsense moral reasoning at the image level—rather than simple keyword blocking—are needed to address this concern. The threat landscape extends further to malicious uses of generative media, where synthetic media detection and attribution technologies must advance in parallel with generative capabilities.

The tendency of diffusion models to memorise training data [arxiv_2305.20086] raises additional concerns about replication of copyrighted content, motivating concept ablation techniques [arxiv_2303.13516] that selectively remove specific styles or identities from pre-trained models without full retraining. Models trained exclusively on Creative Commons-licensed imagery [arxiv_2310.16825] represent a complementary approach, addressing copyright concerns at the data level rather than through post-hoc model editing. Together, bias mitigation, moral content filtering, synthetic media detection, memorization auditing, and data provenance represent complementary and mutually necessary pillars of responsible deployment, each targeting a distinct failure mode that emerges when powerful generative models are released at scale.

## 6. Synthesis and Open Challenges

This survey has traced the development of text-to-image diffusion models across six interconnected dimensions: controllable and personalized generation, text rendering and typography, few-shot and single-image training, synthetic data generation, extension to new modalities, and responsible deployment. Several cross-cutting themes emerge from this review.

First, the tension between generation quality and controllability remains unresolved. While models such as those surveyed in [arxiv_2308.13142] and [arxiv_2308.08157] have achieved remarkable photorealism, fine-grained control over spatial layout [arxiv_2211.14305], attribute binding [arxiv_2212.05032], and semantic alignment [arxiv_2306.09869] continues to require specialized interventions. Attention-level mechanisms [arxiv_2305.13921, arxiv_2308.12964] and grounding-based conditioning [arxiv_2301.07093] have made significant progress, but compositional failures remain a recognized limitation across the field.

Second, inference efficiency represents a persistent bottleneck. The acceleration strategies surveyed in Sections 3.3 and 4.1—spanning optimized noise schedules [arxiv_2106.03802, arxiv_2202.05830], post-hoc noise parameter adjustment [arxiv_2104.02600], retrieval-based trajectory shortening [arxiv_2302.02285], quantization [arxiv_2305.10657], approximate caching [arxiv_2312.04429], and non-autoregressive generation [arxiv_2312.14988]—collectively narrow the gap between diffusion models and faster generative alternatives, but no single approach dominates across all deployment scenarios.

Third, the extension of diffusion models to new modalities—video [arxiv_2303.09535, arxiv_2308.10079], structured markup [arxiv_2210.05147], and typography [arxiv_2303.01818, arxiv_2305.10855]—reveals both the versatility of the diffusion framework and the domain-specific challenges that arise when temporal coherence, structural precision, or character-level fidelity is required. These challenges motivate continued investment in modality-specific conditioning mechanisms and evaluation frameworks.

Fourth, responsible deployment demands attention to memorization [arxiv_2305.20086], copyright [arxiv_2303.13516, arxiv_2310.16825], bias, and synthetic media integrity—concerns that cannot be addressed through generation quality improvements alone and require dedicated research into auditing, filtering, and attribution. The evaluation framework proposed by X-IQE [arxiv_2305.10843] and the explainability agenda it represents point toward a future in which assessment of generative systems is as sophisticated as the systems themselves.

Taken together, the works surveyed here chart a field that has moved rapidly from proof-of-concept synthesis toward practically deployable, user-steerable, and ethically accountable generation systems—while revealing a rich agenda of open problems that will sustain research activity for years to come.
