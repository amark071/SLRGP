# Efficient Inference for Large Language Models

### Surveys and Overviews of LLM Efficient Inference

#### Broad Surveys and Overviews of LLM Efficient Inference

The rapid growth of large language models has generated a substantial body of survey literature aimed at cataloguing and systematizing the diverse techniques proposed to make LLM inference more efficient. These broad overviews collectively reflect the field's recognition that, while LLMs deliver remarkable performance across tasks, their computational and memory demands present serious obstacles to practical deployment [arxiv_2404.14294].

Several surveys approach the problem from complementary angles. Zhou et al. [arxiv_2404.14294] provide a comprehensive taxonomy of inference efficiency techniques, covering algorithmic optimizations, model compression, and hardware-aware strategies, framing the challenge primarily in terms of resource-constrained deployment scenarios. Similarly, Yuan et al. [arxiv_2402.16363] distinguish their contribution by pairing a traditional literature review with a roofline model framework, enabling quantitative analysis of where different methods are bottlenecked by compute or memory bandwidth — an analytical lens largely absent from other surveys. This methodological addition makes [arxiv_2402.16363] particularly useful for practitioners seeking to understand not merely what techniques exist, but why they yield efficiency gains on specific hardware configurations.

Other surveys broaden the scope beyond algorithmic techniques to encompass the full inference serving stack. Zhen et al. [arxiv_2504.19720] focus on the systems-level challenges of serving LLMs at scale, emphasizing the tension between memory overhead from large parameter counts and the computational demands of the attention mechanism, particularly under latency constraints. Pan and Li [arxiv_2506.21901] similarly survey specialized inference systems such as vLLM and SGLang, grounding their analysis in the autoregressive nature of LLM request processing and the system design decisions this motivates. Together, these two works [arxiv_2504.19720, arxiv_2506.21901] complement algorithm-focused surveys by foregrounding throughput, scheduling, and infrastructure concerns that become critical at production scale.

A somewhat different perspective is offered by Liu et al. [arxiv_2401.02038], whose overview spans the full lifecycle from training to inference. While less narrowly focused on inference efficiency than the other surveys, it situates inference optimization within the broader context of cost-efficient LLM development, reflecting the field's growing emphasis on deployment economics. This broader framing is useful for readers seeking to understand how training choices — such as model architecture and scale — propagate into inference costs.

Taken together, these surveys reveal a maturing field in which efficiency is understood as a multi-dimensional problem spanning algorithms, systems, and hardware. The convergence of multiple independent survey efforts [arxiv_2404.14294, arxiv_2402.16363, arxiv_2504.19720, arxiv_2506.21901, arxiv_2401.02038] itself signals the urgency and breadth of the challenge, while the varying emphases across works underscore that no single framework yet provides a fully unified treatment of efficient LLM inference.

#### KV Cache Management, Compression, and Optimization

The key-value (KV) cache has emerged as one of the most critical bottlenecks in large language model inference, and a growing body of survey and empirical literature has sought to systematically characterize the problem and evaluate proposed solutions. At its core, the KV cache eliminates redundant recomputation of past token representations during autoregressive generation, yet its memory footprint scales linearly with context length, imposing severe constraints on GPU memory capacity, bandwidth, and overall throughput as production systems push toward ever-longer contexts [arxiv_2603.20397]. The rapid increase in supported context lengths has driven exponential growth in KV cache memory demand, creating a significant bottleneck that limits both inference efficiency and model scalability [arxiv_2508.06297].

Several survey works have attempted to map the landscape of mitigation strategies. Li et al. provide a broad survey of LLM acceleration techniques organized around KV cache management, situating the problem within the wider computational and memory demands that arise during inference, particularly in long-context and real-time application settings [arxiv_2412.19442]. Complementing this, Liu et al. offer a dedicated review of KV cache compression methods, emphasizing that optimization of the cache during inference is essential for enhancing performance at scale [arxiv_2508.06297]. Xu et al. take a more systems-oriented perspective, framing KV cache optimization as a foundational challenge for scalable deployment and cataloguing strategies that address memory capacity, bandwidth, and throughput simultaneously [arxiv_2603.20397]. Together, these three survey contributions converge on the conclusion that no single technique is universally sufficient, and that effective KV cache management typically requires combining multiple complementary approaches.

Beyond surveys, architectural and algorithmic proposals have sought to operationalize these insights. InfiniGen addresses the challenge of long-content generation by introducing dynamic KV cache management that adapts to sequence length and batch size growth, directly targeting the transient memory footprint that surveys identify as the primary constraint [arxiv_2406.19707]. At the architectural level, the Bounded-Cache Transformer (BCT) proposes building models with a predefined KV cache capacity from the outset, using bounded-length caching within decoder-only attention layers to control memory consumption structurally rather than post hoc [arxiv_2411.15785]. YOCO++ pursues a related goal through cross-layer KV compression with residual connections, aiming to recover the performance degradation that typically accompanies aggressive compression schemes [arxiv_2604.13556]. Whereas BCT and InfiniGen focus primarily on memory bounding and dynamic eviction respectively, YOCO++ targets the quality–efficiency trade-off inherent in cross-layer sharing, illustrating that the field is moving toward solutions that simultaneously address memory, latency, and accuracy objectives identified across the survey literature.

### KV Cache Management and Compression

The rapid expansion of large language model (LLM) capabilities has been accompanied by a growing inference bottleneck rooted in the memory demands of the key-value (KV) cache. As context lengths increase, KV cache memory consumption grows substantially, constraining both throughput and scalability in real-world deployments. This challenge has motivated a substantial body of research, comprehensively surveyed by [arxiv_2412.19442] and [arxiv_2508.06297], both of which frame KV cache management as central to efficient LLM inference. While [arxiv_2412.19442] situates KV cache optimization within the broader landscape of LLM acceleration—spanning natural language processing, computer vision, and multimodal tasks—[arxiv_2508.06297] focuses more narrowly on compression strategies, cataloguing the exponential growth in caching demand as a primary driver of memory bottlenecks that limit model scalability.

Architectural responses to this challenge have taken several forms. One prominent direction involves restructuring how KV pairs are allocated across transformer layers. [arxiv_2405.10637] proposes a layer-condensed KV cache design in which only a subset of layers maintain full KV representations, substantially reducing memory overhead in deep language models where the number of layers amplifies cache size. This approach targets the architectural source of the problem rather than compressing existing caches post hoc. A complementary architectural strategy is introduced by [arxiv_2411.15785], which presents the Bounded-Cache Transformer (BCT), a framework for constructing LLMs with a predefined KV cache capacity from the outset. By enforcing a bounded-length cache through dynamic mechanisms within decoder-only transformer architectures, BCT offers a principled means of controlling memory consumption at design time rather than at inference time. Together, these two approaches illustrate a shift toward proactive architectural constraints as an alternative to reactive compression.

At the systems level, individual optimization techniques rarely operate in isolation, and their interactions can be non-trivial. [arxiv_2512.01953] addresses this gap by examining the joint effects of KV cache quantization, chunked prefill, and model weight quantization under a unified framework called KV Pareto, designed specifically for long-context inference in edge deployment scenarios. The authors observe that while each technique individually demonstrates promise, their combined configurations and trade-offs remain underexplored—a finding that underscores the need for holistic, systems-aware optimization strategies rather than isolated algorithmic improvements. Collectively, this body of work reveals a field progressing from isolated compression heuristics toward integrated architectural and systems-level solutions capable of addressing the memory demands of increasingly long-context LLM inference.
