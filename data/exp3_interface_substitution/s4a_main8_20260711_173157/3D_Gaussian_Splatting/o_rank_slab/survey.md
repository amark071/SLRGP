# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Rank slab 1: positions 1-8

The foundational contribution to 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) was introduced by Kerbl et al. [arxiv_2308.04079], who proposed representing scenes as sets of optimizable 3D Gaussian ellipsoids that can be rendered in real time at 1080p resolution without sacrificing visual quality. This work addressed a longstanding tension in novel view synthesis between rendering speed and photorealistic output, a trade-off that had persistently constrained earlier neural implicit approaches such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). As surveyed comprehensively by Wu et al. [arxiv_2403.11134], the emergence of 3DGS marked a significant acceleration in rendering speed relative to NeRF-based methods, which rely on position- and viewpoint-conditioned neural networks and volumetric rendering pipelines that are computationally expensive both to train and to query.

Despite its considerable strengths, the original 3DGS formulation carries notable limitations that subsequent work has sought to address. One prominent concern is memory consumption: because 3DGS stores per-Gaussian parameters to model view-dependent appearance and anisotropic geometry, the memory overhead can be substantial. Wang and Xu [arxiv_2509.17083] explicitly identify this as a central drawback of the explicit Gaussian representation and propose hybrid radiance field strategies to mitigate it. Similarly, Girish et al. [arxiv_2312.04564] observe that while 3DGS achieves real-time rendering through rapid differentiable rasterization, the approach demands significant memory resources, motivating their work on lightweight encodings to compress Gaussian attributes without degrading rendering quality.

A separate line of inquiry concerns the theoretical underpinnings of the 3DGS rendering pipeline. Celarek et al. [arxiv_2502.19318] examine whether 3DGS genuinely requires accurate volumetric rendering, noting that unlike NeRF's principled ray-marching formulation, 3DGS employs an approximation that nonetheless achieves high visual fidelity and fast training times. This raises important questions about the relationship between rendering correctness and perceptual quality in learned scene representations.

Further extensions of 3DGS have targeted robustness to imperfect input data. Lee et al. [arxiv_2401.00834] adapt the Gaussian splatting framework to handle motion-blurred images, enabling high-quality novel view synthesis in real-time applications where sharp inputs cannot be guaranteed. Zhao et al. [arxiv_2403.11831] pursue a related goal through bundle-adjusted deblurring within the Gaussian splatting paradigm, addressing both motion blur and inaccurate camera poses, conditions frequently encountered in low-light or long-exposure capture scenarios. Finally, Song et al. [arxiv_2411.01853] extend 3DGS toward surface reconstruction in open scenes, contrasting the explicit and discrete nature of Gaussian representations with the implicit formulations of NeRF-based reconstruction methods, which incur substantially greater training and rendering costs.

### Rank slab 2: positions 9-15

Research into 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has rapidly expanded beyond its foundational formulation, with a growing body of work addressing its practical limitations across storage, dynamic scenes, manipulation, and rendering fidelity. A central concern that has emerged in the literature is the substantial memory footprint incurred by representing scenes with millions of learnable Gaussian primitives. Both [arxiv_2408.03822] and [arxiv_2502.19457] foreground this problem directly. Lee et al. [arxiv_2408.03822] propose compact representations applicable to both static and dynamic radiance fields, observing that while 3DGS achieves fast rendering and promising image quality, the sheer number of Gaussians required constitutes a significant practical barrier. This concern is systematically catalogued by Ali et al. [arxiv_2502.19457], whose survey of compression methods situates the memory challenge within the broader context of 3DGS's differentiable rendering pipeline and its departure from the implicit, coordinate-based paradigm of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). Together, these two works frame compression not as a peripheral optimisation but as a foundational requirement for deployment.

A second thread in this rank concerns the extension of 3DGS to dynamic scenes. Lee et al. [arxiv_2408.03822] explicitly address dynamic 3D scenes as a natural extension of their compact framework, demonstrating the versatility of Gaussian-based representations beyond static environments. This concern with temporal dynamics connects to earlier work in the broader slab, where geometry-aware deformation has been identified as a key challenge [arxiv_2404.06270], reinforcing the view that dynamic scene modelling requires principled geometric grounding rather than purely implicit deformation.

The question of scene manipulation and editability is taken up by Gao et al. [arxiv_2405.17811], who argue that while NeRF-based methods excel at photorealistic rendering, they lack the flexibility required for content creation workflows. Their Mani-GS framework couples Gaussian representations with triangular meshes to enable fine-grained manipulation, positioning 3DGS as a more tractable substrate for editing than implicit neural models. This aligns with the broader characterisation offered in survey-level treatments [arxiv_2401.03890], which identify editability as one of the transformative advantages of explicit Gaussian representations over mainstream implicit neural models.

Taken together, the works in this rank reveal a field actively negotiating the trade-offs inherent to explicit Gaussian representations: the memory costs of scale, the geometric demands of dynamic content, and the structural requirements of interactive manipulation. The convergence of compression research [arxiv_2502.19457], compact dynamic modelling [arxiv_2408.03822], and mesh-coupled editing [arxiv_2405.17811] suggests that the community is moving toward hybrid and constrained formulations that preserve the rendering speed of 3DGS while addressing its practical shortcomings in real-world applications.
