# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Foundational Methods and Surveys of 3DGS

Three-dimensional Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) was introduced by Kerbl et al. as a real-time radiance field rendering method capable of achieving high visual quality for unbounded and complete scenes at 1080p resolution [arxiv_2308.04079]. Prior to its introduction, the dominant paradigm for novel-view synthesis was Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), which relied on position- and viewpoint-conditioned neural networks and a principled ray-marching approach for volumetric rendering. While NeRF-based methods produced compelling results, they incurred substantial computational costs during both training and inference, and faster variants typically sacrificed visual quality to achieve speed gains [arxiv_2308.04079]. The emergence of 3DGS addressed these limitations by representing scenes as collections of 3D Gaussian ellipsoids rather than implicit neural functions, enabling efficient rasterization-based rendering that bypasses the expensive ray-marching procedures characteristic of NeRF [arxiv_2403.11134].

The relationship between 3DGS and volumetric rendering theory has attracted subsequent scholarly attention. Celarek et al. examined whether 3DGS actually adheres to accurate volumetric rendering principles, noting that while 3DGS shares surface-level similarities with NeRF's rendering formulation, its underlying approximations diverge meaningfully from a physically principled volumetric treatment [arxiv_2502.19318]. This theoretical investigation is significant because it clarifies that the empirical success of 3DGS cannot be straightforwardly attributed to volumetric rendering correctness, raising important questions about why the method generalizes as well as it does in practice.

The broader impact of 3DGS on the field has been documented in several survey works. Wu et al. provide a comprehensive review of advances built upon the original method, emphasizing that 3DGS has greatly accelerated rendering speeds for novel-view synthesis and has stimulated a wide range of downstream extensions [arxiv_2403.11134]. This survey situates 3DGS within the landscape of 3D scene representations, contrasting its explicit Gaussian-based formulation with the implicit neural representations that preceded it. From a wider historical perspective, Gao et al. contextualize 3DGS as a direct competitor to the NeRF framework, noting that its introduction in August 2023 marked a significant inflection point in the field of 3D vision, with the method rapidly attracting attention across applications including robotics, urban mapping, and augmented reality [arxiv_2210.00379]. Together, these foundational and survey works establish 3DGS as a methodologically distinct and practically impactful paradigm, one whose theoretical underpinnings continue to be interrogated even as its empirical utility drives rapid community adoption.

### Compression, Efficiency, and Memory Optimization

#### Surveys and Overviews of 3DGS Compression and Advances

The rapid ascent of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a dominant paradigm for real-time novel view synthesis has been accompanied by a growing body of survey literature that attempts to systematize the field's compression and efficiency advances. The foundational method, introduced by Kerbl et al. [arxiv_2308.04079], represents scenes as millions of learnable 3D Gaussian primitives rendered via differentiable rasterization, achieving real-time performance at 1080p resolution. However, this expressive power comes at a steep storage cost, with individual scene reconstructions routinely demanding gigabytes of memory [arxiv_2504.03059], a bottleneck that has motivated an entire sub-discipline of compression research.

Several survey works have emerged to consolidate this rapidly expanding literature. Ali et al. [arxiv_2502.19457] provide a comprehensive taxonomy of 3DGS compression methods, situating them within the broader context of explicit scene representations and contrasting the approach with implicit neural radiance field (NeRF) methods. Their survey traces methodological trends and identifies future research directions, offering a structured view of how the community has responded to the memory demands inherent in storing millions of per-Gaussian parameters. Similarly, Wu et al. [arxiv_2403.11134] survey recent advances in 3DGS more broadly, emphasizing how the shift from coordinate-based neural networks to explicit Gaussian ellipsoids has fundamentally altered the rendering speed landscape, while also acknowledging the scalability challenges this representation introduces. Complementing these efforts, Bagdasarian et al. [arxiv_2407.09510] focus specifically on compression, cataloguing the state-of-the-art in terms of both quality and speed trade-offs. Their survey, titled 3DGS.zip, is particularly attentive to the tension between rendering fidelity and model compactness, noting that despite 3DGS's advantages in throughput, its storage footprint remains a critical barrier to practical deployment.

Taken together, these surveys reveal several converging themes in the compression literature. A primary concern is the reduction of the number of Gaussian primitives without sacrificing visual quality, a challenge addressed through pruning, quantization, and compact encoding strategies [arxiv_2406.17074], [arxiv_2311.17245]. A secondary concern involves the efficient encoding of per-Gaussian attributes—including spherical harmonic coefficients, opacity, and covariance parameters—through techniques such as vector quantization [arxiv_2504.03059], locality-aware neural field representations [arxiv_2501.05757], and lightweight encoding schemes [arxiv_2312.04564]. More recent work has also questioned whether the per-scene optimization bottleneck that characterizes most compression pipelines can be circumvented through feedforward approaches [arxiv_2410.08017], while hybrid representations combining explicit Gaussians with implicit components have been proposed to reduce per-Gaussian parameter counts [arxiv_2509.17083]. The survey literature thus not only documents these individual contributions but also frames the overarching challenge: balancing the expressive, real-time advantages of 3DGS against the memory and transmission costs that limit its scalability, a tension that [arxiv_2502.19318] further complicates by questioning the volumetric rendering assumptions underlying the method's design.

#### Quantization and Encoding-based Compression

Quantization and encoding-based compression have emerged as prominent strategies for reducing the substantial memory footprint of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), with several works proposing distinct but complementary approaches to this challenge. A central motivation across this body of research is the observation that representing a single scene with millions of Gaussian splats, each parameterized by 59 floating-point values, can demand approximately 1 GB of storage, rendering deployment in memory-constrained environments highly impractical [arxiv_2504.03059].

Vector quantization and codebook-based methods represent one major direction. Wang et al. propose a noise-substituted vector quantization scheme that targets the redundancy inherent in the large parameter sets of individual Gaussians, compressing scene representations without sacrificing rendering fidelity [arxiv_2504.03059]. Similarly, Lee et al. introduce a compact 3DGS representation that addresses both storage and computational efficiency, recognizing that while 3DGS offers advantages over Neural Radiance Fields in terms of rendering speed, its memory demands remain a significant barrier to widespread adoption [arxiv_2311.13681]. Both works share the insight that the attributes of Gaussian primitives exhibit structured redundancy amenable to quantization, though they differ in the specific mechanisms employed to exploit this redundancy.

Lightweight encoding of Gaussian attributes constitutes another closely related approach. Girish et al. present EAGLES, which combines efficient attribute encodings with accelerated training to reduce memory consumption during both storage and rendering [arxiv_2312.04564]. In contrast to methods that apply quantization as a post-hoc compression step, EAGLES integrates lightweight encoding directly into the representation learning process, aiming to achieve memory efficiency without incurring additional per-scene optimization overhead.

A distinct limitation shared by many of these quantization and encoding approaches is their reliance on per-scene optimization to achieve compression, which can be computationally expensive and time-consuming. Chen et al. directly address this bottleneck by proposing a fast feedforward compression pipeline for 3DGS that eliminates the need for scene-specific re-optimization, enabling significantly faster compression while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality [arxiv_2410.08017]. This feedforward paradigm represents a meaningful departure from prior art and suggests a promising direction for scalable deployment of compressed 3DGS models.

Collectively, these works illustrate a progression from quantization-centric methods that prioritize storage reduction [arxiv_2504.03059, arxiv_2311.13681], through integrated encoding strategies that target training-time efficiency [arxiv_2312.04564], toward inference-time compression pipelines that prioritize speed and scalability [arxiv_2410.08017], reflecting the field's growing awareness that practical deployment demands both compact representations and efficient compression workflows.
