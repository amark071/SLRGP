# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Foundations and Core 3DGS Methods

Three-dimensional Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a landmark approach in real-time novel-view synthesis, addressing longstanding trade-offs between rendering speed and visual fidelity that had constrained earlier radiance field methods. Kerbl et al. introduced the foundational framework, representing scenes as collections of 3D Gaussian primitives that can be rasterized efficiently, enabling high-quality rendering at 1080p resolution in real time without the costly neural network inference required by Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) [arxiv_2308.04079]. The method achieves fast training times alongside competitive visual quality for unbounded and complete scenes, establishing 3DGS as a central reference point for subsequent work in learned 3D scene representations [arxiv_2308.04079].

Despite its practical success, the theoretical underpinnings of 3DGS rendering have attracted scrutiny. Celarek et al. observe that while NeRF is grounded in a principled ray-marching volumetric rendering framework, 3DGS shares a similar appearance but departs from strict volumetric rendering assumptions [arxiv_2502.19318]. Their analysis interrogates whether the approximations embedded in 3DGS's splatting pipeline are necessary for its performance or whether more accurate volumetric rendering would yield meaningful improvements, providing important theoretical context for understanding why the method works as well as it does in practice [arxiv_2502.19318].

Alongside this theoretical scrutiny, researchers have explored alternative primitive representations that address known limitations of Gaussian primitives. Franke et al. propose TRIPS, a trilinear point splatting approach motivated in part by the observation that 3DGS struggles with highly detailed scenes, exhibiting blurring and cloudy artifacts when fine-grained geometry must be captured [arxiv_2401.06003]. Similarly, Held et al. introduce 3D Convex Splatting, replacing Gaussian primitives with smooth convex shapes to better handle scene reconstruction challenges such as accurately representing hard edges, which require a disproportionately large number of Gaussians under the standard formulation [arxiv_2411.14974]. Both alternatives thus respond to complementary failure modes of the original 3DGS framework: TRIPS targets detail and sharpness degradation [arxiv_2401.06003], while 3D Convex Splatting targets geometric expressiveness at boundaries [arxiv_2411.14974]. Together, these works illustrate how the foundational 3DGS method [arxiv_2308.04079] has catalysed a productive line of inquiry into both the theoretical justification of its rendering assumptions and the practical design of more expressive scene primitives.

### Memory Efficiency and Compression

The rapid adoption of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has been tempered by a well-documented limitation: the method's reliance on millions of learnable Gaussian primitives incurs substantial memory and storage costs, often reaching gigabyte-level disk space for a single unbounded scene [arxiv_2311.17245]. Comprehensive surveys of the field confirm that memory efficiency and compression have consequently become central research priorities [arxiv_2502.19457, arxiv_2407.09510, arxiv_2403.11134].

One prominent line of work addresses this problem through pruning and sparsification. LightGaussian [arxiv_2311.17245] demonstrates that a large fraction of Gaussians contribute negligibly to final rendering quality and can be removed, achieving roughly a 15× reduction in model size while maintaining real-time framerates. Similarly, Ali et al. [arxiv_2406.18214] propose a structured pruning strategy that trims redundant Gaussian attributes, showing that aggressive sparsification need not substantially degrade visual fidelity. More recently, MEGS² [arxiv_2509.07021] extends this direction by coupling pruning with a spherical Gaussian representation, explicitly targeting rendering-time memory rather than storage alone—a distinction the authors argue is overlooked by most prior compression methods.

Quantization and entropy coding constitute a complementary strategy. EAGLES [arxiv_2312.04564] introduces lightweight encodings that quantize Gaussian attributes, reducing memory requirements for both training and inference. Compact3D [arxiv_2311.13681] further combines quantization with codebook-based compression, achieving compact representations without sacrificing reconstruction quality. Xu et al. [arxiv_2509.13482] advance this direction with scene-adaptive lattice vector quantization, arguing that fixed quantization grids fail to capture the non-uniform distribution of Gaussian attributes across different scenes, and demonstrating improved rate-distortion performance over anchor-based neural compression baselines.

Spatial coherence among neighboring Gaussians offers another avenue for compactness. Papantonakis et al. [arxiv_2406.17074] systematically analyze the sources of memory overhead and propose targeted reductions across primitive count, per-primitive attribute precision, and transmission cost. LocoGS [arxiv_2501.05757] builds on this insight more explicitly, encoding locally coherent Gaussian attributes via a neural field representation that exploits spatial redundancy, yielding high compression ratios alongside fast rendering. Hybrid approaches also show promise: HyRF [arxiv_2509.17083] combines explicit Gaussians with implicit radiance field components to reduce the per-Gaussian parameter burden associated with view-dependent appearance modeling.

A further architectural distinction concerns whether compression requires per-scene optimization. Chen et al. [arxiv_2410.08017] identify this as a critical bottleneck in prior art and propose a feedforward compression network that generalizes across scenes, substantially reducing compression time. Taken together, these works reveal a field progressing along multiple simultaneous axes—sparsification, quantization, structural coherence, and amortized inference—with ongoing tension between compression ratio, rendering speed, and visual quality [arxiv_2502.19457, arxiv_2407.09510].
