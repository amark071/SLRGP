# Multimodal Large Language Models

### Injected invalid unbalanced organization

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen significant progress in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for systems capable of integrating and processing diverse modalities such as text, images, audio, and video [arxiv_2410.05608]. These models aim to bridge the gap between unimodal representations and the complex, real-world scenarios where multiple modalities coexist. However, despite their promise, challenges persist in ensuring balanced integration and effective organization of multimodal inputs. This subsection reviews the literature on MLLMs with a focus on the issues arising from injected invalid unbalanced organization, highlighting the implications for model performance and adaptability.

One of the foundational works in this area is the survey by Caffagni et al. [arxiv_2402.12451], who emphasize the importance of connecting text and visual modalities in generative intelligence. They argue that MLLMs must seamlessly integrate these modalities while providing a dialogue-based interface and instruction-following capabilities. However, the survey does not explicitly address the challenges posed by invalid or unbalanced input structures. This omission highlights a gap in the literature, where the focus often remains on the capabilities of MLLMs rather than the limitations imposed by flawed input organization.

In contrast, Li et al. [arxiv_2309.10020] provide a more comprehensive taxonomy of multimodal foundation models, categorizing them into specialist and general-purpose assistants. They note that the transition from specialist models to general-purpose assistants involves overcoming several challenges, including the integration of diverse modalities and the handling of complex input structures. While the paper acknowledges the importance of balanced multimodal inputs, it does not delve into the specific issues arising from injected invalid unbalanced organization. Instead, it focuses on broader trends in multimodal research, such as the evolution from vision-language pre-training (VLP) to more generalized models.

The evolution of VLP methods is further explored by Gan et al. [arxiv_2210.09263], who categorize these approaches into three main areas: image-text tasks, core computer vision tasks, and multimodal reasoning tasks. They highlight the advancements in techniques such as image captioning, visual question answering, and visual grounding. However, the paper does not address the implications of unbalanced or invalid input structures on model performance. This oversight suggests that while VLP methods have improved significantly, the robustness of these models in handling malformed inputs remains underexplored.

The tutorial by Han et al. [arxiv_2410.05608] provides a broader perspective on multimodal models, covering foundational concepts, the evolution of multimodal research, and key technical challenges. They emphasize the need for models that can integrate and process diverse data forms effectively. However, the tutorial does not explicitly discuss the impact of injected invalid unbalanced organization on model performance. Instead, it focuses on the latest advancements in multimodal pretrained models and their applications across various domains.

Ebrahimi et al. [arxiv_2408.06610] introduce CROME, a cross-modal adapter designed to enhance the efficiency of MLLMs. They argue that existing approaches often require expensive retraining of language models and offer limited adaptability. While the paper addresses the challenges of cost-effective training and adaptation, it does not specifically examine the effects of invalid or unbalanced input structures. Instead, it focuses on improving zero-shot performance and task-specific tuning, highlighting the need for more flexible adaptation strategies.

Chen et al. [arxiv_2209.06794] present PaLI, a jointly-scaled multilingual language-image model that extends the capabilities of large language models to joint modeling of language and vision. They emphasize the importance of effective scaling and a flexible task interface. However, the paper does not address the challenges posed by invalid or unbalanced input structures. Instead, it focuses on the model's ability to perform a wide range of tasks across multiple languages, underscoring the importance of scalability and flexibility in multimodal models.

Liu et al. [ss_478472] propose VALOR, a vision-audio-language omni-perception pretraining model that jointly models the relationships among vision, audio, and language. Unlike traditional vision-language pretraining models, VALOR integrates multiple modalities in an end-to-end manner. The paper highlights the importance of modeling complex relationships among modalities but does not explicitly address the challenges posed by invalid or unbalanced input structures. Instead, it focuses on the model's ability to handle diverse inputs and perform multimodal tasks effectively.

Chawla et al. [arxiv_2403.08773] discuss advancements in multimodal representation learning, emphasizing the integration of textual and visual information. They argue that multimodal models extend the capabilities of large language models to address a diverse array of tasks. However, the paper does not delve into the specific challenges posed by invalid or unbalanced input structures. Instead, it focuses on the broader advancements in multimodal models and their applications across various domains.

In summary, while the reviewed literature highlights significant advancements in MLLMs, there is a notable lack of focus on the challenges posed by injected invalid unbalanced organization. Most papers emphasize the capabilities and evolution of multimodal models, often overlooking the implications of flawed input structures on model performance and adaptability. Future research should address these challenges to ensure the robustness and reliability of MLLMs in real-world applications.
