# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Foundational Methods and Surveys of 3DGS

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a transformative method in the field of novel view synthesis, offering a compelling alternative to neural implicit representations such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) [2301.03516]. Unlike NeRF, which relies on position- and viewpoint-conditioned neural networks to represent scenes implicitly, 3DGS employs a set of Gaussian ellipsoids to model the spatial distribution of radiance in a scene. This explicit representation allows for efficient rendering and significantly accelerates the synthesis of novel views, making it particularly suitable for real-time applications [arxiv_2403.11134].

The foundational work on 3DGS has drawn comparisons with NeRF, particularly in terms of rendering accuracy and computational efficiency. While NeRF relies on a principled ray-marching approach for volumetric rendering, 3DGS achieves comparable visual quality with faster rendering times by leveraging Gaussian-based splatting techniques [arxiv_2502.19318]. This efficiency has made 3DGS a popular choice for applications requiring real-time performance, such as virtual reality and augmented reality systems [arxiv_2308.04079].

Despite its advantages, the question of whether 3DGS requires accurate volumetric rendering remains a topic of investigation. Some studies suggest that while volumetric accuracy is beneficial, it may not be strictly necessary for achieving high visual quality in novel view synthesis [arxiv_2502.19318]. This insight opens avenues for further optimization and simplification of the rendering pipeline, potentially expanding the applicability of 3DGS to more complex and unbounded scenes.

The emergence of 3DGS has prompted a reevaluation of NeRF-based frameworks. A comprehensive review of NeRF highlights its foundational role in advancing 3D vision and novel view synthesis, while also acknowledging the disruptive potential of Gaussian splatting techniques [arxiv_2210.00379]. NeRF's implicit representation has enabled diverse applications in robotics, autonomous navigation, and immersive technologies. However, the explicit nature of 3DGS offers a more scalable and computationally efficient solution, particularly for real-time rendering at high resolutions [arxiv_2308.04079].

In summary, 3DGS represents a significant advancement in the field of 3D scene representation and rendering. By leveraging Gaussian ellipsoids, it provides a balance between visual fidelity and computational efficiency that complements and, in some cases, surpasses traditional NeRF-based methods. As research continues to explore the capabilities and limitations of 3DGS, it is likely to influence the development of future rendering pipelines and expand the scope of applications in real-time 3D vision systems.

### Compression, Efficiency, and Memory Optimization

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a promising alternative to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) for novel view synthesis, offering real-time rendering capabilities and reduced training times [arxiv_2312.04564]. However, despite its advantages in speed and quality, 3DGS faces significant challenges related to memory consumption and computational efficiency, which limit its scalability and deployment in resource-constrained environments [arxiv_2405.17083]. Several recent works have sought to address these limitations through various compression strategies and optimization techniques.

One notable approach is proposed in HyRF by Zipeng Wang and Dan Xu [arxiv_2509.17083], which introduces Hybrid Radiance Fields to reduce memory overhead while maintaining high-quality rendering. HyRF leverages a combination of explicit Gaussian parameters and implicit representations to model view-dependent effects and anisotropic shapes more efficiently than traditional 3DGS methods. This hybrid strategy allows for a reduction in memory usage without compromising visual fidelity, making it suitable for applications requiring both quality and efficiency.

In parallel, Sharath Girish et al. introduce EAGLES [arxiv_2312.04564], which focuses on optimizing both training speed and rendering performance through lightweight encoding schemes. While EAGLES achieves real-time rendering and accelerated training, it still requires substantial memory resources due to the explicit storage of per-Gaussian parameters. This highlights a key challenge in 3DGS: balancing rendering quality with memory efficiency.

To further address these issues, Xiangyu Sun et al. propose F-3DGS [arxiv_2405.17083], which introduces factorized coordinates and representations to reduce redundancy in Gaussian parameters. By decomposing the spatial and appearance components of Gaussians, F-3DGS achieves more compact representations while preserving rendering quality. This approach is particularly effective in reducing memory footprints, enabling deployment on devices with limited resources.

Comparing these methods, HyRF and F-3DGS both aim to reduce memory overhead through structural modifications to Gaussian representations, whereas EAGLES focuses on lightweight encoding strategies. While HyRF emphasizes hybrid modeling for view-dependent effects, F-3DGS prioritizes parameter factorization to achieve compactness. These approaches demonstrate different strategies for optimizing memory usage in 3DGS, each with trade-offs between efficiency and rendering fidelity.

In addition to memory optimization, rendering quality remains a critical concern. Byeonghyeon Lee et al. [arxiv_2401.00834] address the issue of motion blur and temporal inconsistencies in 3DGS-based rendering through a deblurring framework. Their work highlights the importance of maintaining high-quality output while optimizing performance, suggesting that future research should consider both efficiency and visual accuracy in tandem.

In summary, recent advances in 3DGS have focused on reducing memory overhead and computational costs through hybrid modeling, lightweight encoding, and parameter factorization. While each approach offers distinct advantages, ongoing research is needed to further optimize these methods for broader deployment in real-time applications.
