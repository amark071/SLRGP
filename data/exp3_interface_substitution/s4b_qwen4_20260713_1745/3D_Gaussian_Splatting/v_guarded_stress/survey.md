# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Foundational Methods and Surveys of 3DGS

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has significantly advanced the field of novel view synthesis by offering a balance between rendering speed and visual quality [arxiv_2403.11134]. Unlike Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), which rely on position and viewpoint-conditioned neural networks to represent scenes implicitly, 3DGS employs a set of Gaussian ellipsoids to model the scene, enabling efficient rendering [arxiv_2403.11134]. This method has been particularly notable for its ability to achieve real-time rendering performance while maintaining high visual fidelity, a challenge that traditional radiance field methods have struggled with [arxiv_2308.04079].

The foundational work in 3DGS was introduced as a direct competitor to NeRF-based frameworks, offering a more computationally efficient alternative [arxiv_2210.00379]. While NeRFs are based on a principled ray-marching approach for volumetric rendering, 3DGS diverges by utilizing Gaussian ellipsoids to represent the scene geometry and radiance. This approach allows for faster training times and real-time rendering capabilities, making it particularly suitable for applications requiring dynamic interactions or large-scale scene representations [arxiv_2502.19318]. Despite these advantages, the question of whether accurate volumetric rendering is necessary for 3DGS remains an active area of investigation [arxiv_2502.19318].

One of the key contributions of 3DGS is its ability to render complete scenes at high resolutions, such as 1080p, without compromising visual quality [arxiv_2308.04079]. This is particularly significant given the limitations of earlier methods, which often required trade-offs between speed and quality. The use of Gaussian ellipsoids allows for efficient spatial partitioning and rendering, which is crucial for handling unbounded scenes [arxiv_2308.04079]. This efficiency has made 3DGS a popular choice for applications ranging from virtual reality to autonomous navigation, where real-time performance is essential [arxiv_2210.00379].

Comparative studies between 3DGS and NeRF highlight the strengths and limitations of each approach. While NeRFs offer a principled and flexible framework for scene representation, they are computationally intensive, requiring extensive training times and rendering resources [arxiv_2502.19318]. In contrast, 3DGS provides a more scalable solution, particularly for large-scale and real-time applications [arxiv_2403.11134]. However, the trade-off between accuracy and efficiency remains a critical consideration, as recent investigations suggest that the need for accurate volumetric rendering in 3DGS is still an open question [arxiv_2502.19318].

In summary, 3DGS represents a significant advancement in the field of novel view synthesis, offering a practical alternative to traditional radiance field methods. Its efficiency and scalability make it a promising approach for a wide range of applications, although ongoing research is needed to fully understand its capabilities and limitations compared to established methods like NeRF [arxiv_2210.00379].

### Compression, Efficiency, and Memory Optimization

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a promising alternative to Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) has sparked significant interest due to its ability to enable real-time novel view synthesis with high quality [arxiv_2312.04564]. However, despite its advantages in rendering speed and training efficiency, 3DGS faces notable challenges related to memory consumption and computational efficiency, primarily due to the explicit modeling of per-Gaussian parameters [arxiv_2509.17083]. These challenges have prompted recent research efforts aimed at optimizing compression, efficiency, and memory usage in 3DGS systems.

One notable approach is the work by Wang and Xu, who introduce Hybrid Radiance Fields (HyRF) to reduce memory overhead while maintaining high-quality rendering [arxiv_2509.17083]. HyRF addresses the inefficiency of traditional 3DGS by optimizing how view-dependent effects and anisotropic shapes are modeled. Their method leverages a hybrid approach that combines explicit Gaussian parameters with implicit representations to reduce redundancy and improve memory efficiency. This is particularly important given that standard 3DGS implementations require storing large numbers of Gaussian parameters, which can be prohibitive in memory-constrained environments.

In parallel, the EAGLES framework proposed by Sharath Girish et al. focuses on accelerating both training and rendering while maintaining lightweight encoding strategies [arxiv_2312.04564]. By leveraging efficient differentiable rasterization techniques, EAGLES achieves real-time performance without sacrificing rendering fidelity. Their approach emphasizes the importance of optimizing Gaussian encoding strategies to reduce computational overhead while maintaining high-quality outputs. This aligns with broader efforts to make 3DGS more scalable and deployable in real-world applications.

Another significant contribution comes from Sun et al., who introduce Factorized Coordinates and Representations (F-3DGS) to enhance the efficiency of 3DGS [arxiv_2405.17083]. Their method reduces computational costs by factorizing Gaussian parameters into more compact representations, allowing for faster rendering and lower memory usage. This is particularly relevant for deployment in resource-constrained environments where computational efficiency is critical. F-3DGS demonstrates that factorization techniques can significantly improve the scalability of 3DGS without compromising rendering quality.

Finally, Lee et al. explore the issue of blurring artifacts in 3DGS and propose a deblurring framework that enhances rendering clarity while maintaining efficiency [arxiv_2401.00834]. Their work highlights the trade-off between rendering speed and visual fidelity, emphasizing the need for optimization strategies that balance these factors. By refining Gaussian splatting techniques, their method contributes to the broader goal of making 3DGS more practical for real-time applications.

Taken together, these works illustrate a growing trend toward optimizing compression, efficiency, and memory usage in 3DGS. While each approach addresses different aspects of the problem, they collectively underscore the importance of balancing computational efficiency with rendering quality to enable broader adoption of 3DGS in real-world applications.
