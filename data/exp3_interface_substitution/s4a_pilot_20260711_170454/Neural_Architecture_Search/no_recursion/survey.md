# Neural Architecture Search

### Surveys and Overviews of NAS

The automation of neural network design has emerged as a central concern in modern deep learning research, giving rise to the field of Neural Architecture Search (NAS). Early comprehensive treatments of the area, such as the survey by Wistuba, Rawat, and colleagues, documented how the growing interest in automating machine learning had produced a wide variety of methods for discovering effective network architectures, noting that architectural choices are critical to performance and that deep learning techniques are computationally intensive [arxiv_1905.01392]. This foundational observation—that manual architecture engineering is both consequential and costly—motivates the entire NAS research programme.

Subsequent overviews have sought to organise the expanding literature into coherent taxonomies. Kim and colleagues classify the main research trends in NAS into three broad families: neuro-evolutionary algorithms, reinforcement learning-based algorithms, and one-shot architecture search approaches [arxiv_2108.08474]. This tripartite classification reflects the historical trajectory of the field, in which evolutionary and reinforcement learning methods preceded the more recent gradient-based paradigms. The same work emphasises acceleration as a unifying concern across all three families, underscoring that computational cost remains a persistent bottleneck even as methods have matured [arxiv_2108.08474].

Reinforcement learning-based NAS has attracted dedicated survey attention in its own right. Shao's focused review situates RL-based NAS within the broader challenge of automating the exploration of optimal network structures and hyperparameters, a process that otherwise demands extensive and tedious human intervention [arxiv_2409.18163]. By concentrating on a single search paradigm, this work complements broader overviews such as those of Wistuba et al. [arxiv_1905.01392] and Kim et al. [arxiv_2108.08474], providing greater depth on the mechanisms and limitations specific to RL-driven search.

A more recent strand of survey literature addresses differentiable NAS (DNAS) as a distinct and increasingly dominant paradigm. Heuillet and colleagues argue that DNAS rapidly imposed itself as the trending approach to automated architecture discovery, attributing its rise largely to the popularity of DARTS as one of the first major differentiable methods [arxiv_2304.05405]. Crucially, they contrast DNAS with prior RL and evolutionary approaches, noting that differentiable methods are faster by several orders of magnitude [arxiv_2304.05405]. This efficiency advantage explains why DNAS has attracted dedicated survey treatment separate from general NAS overviews. Taken together, these surveys reveal a field that has progressed from computationally prohibitive search procedures toward increasingly efficient, gradient-amenable formulations, while retaining diversity in its methodological foundations.

### Reinforcement Learning-based NAS

Reinforcement learning-based neural architecture search (RL-based NAS) represents one of the foundational paradigms in automated architecture design, wherein a controller agent learns to propose and refine network architectures by receiving performance-based reward signals. A comprehensive survey of this paradigm is provided by [arxiv_2409.18163], which traces how RL-based NAS emerged as a response to the prohibitive human effort involved in manually tuning deep neural network structures and hyperparameters. The core appeal of the approach lies in its ability to frame architecture discovery as a sequential decision-making problem, yet this framing historically entailed enormous computational cost, a limitation that has motivated much of the subsequent methodological development in the field.

A central bottleneck in RL-based NAS is the rollout problem, whereby each sampled architecture must be trained to convergence on a proxy task before a reward signal can be computed. [arxiv_2105.11694] directly addresses this issue by proposing an uncertainty-aware pipeline that accelerates rollout convergence, thereby reducing the resource demands that have traditionally distinguished RL-based methods unfavorably from gradient-based alternatives. Similarly, [arxiv_2001.06679] introduces a broad scalable architecture combined with parameter sharing inspired by ENAS, achieving faster training through a shallow topology while retaining the RL optimization framework. These efforts collectively reflect a sustained effort to close the efficiency gap between RL-based and differentiable NAS approaches.

Beyond raw efficiency, researchers have explored structural innovations within the RL search process itself. [arxiv_1910.10397] proposes decoupling the search over network structure from the search over operations, sampling each from independent multinomial distributions, which substantially reduces the effective search space and improves convergence. [arxiv_2110.06751] further investigates sample efficiency, arguing that smarter use of each architecture evaluation can meaningfully reduce the number of rollouts required. In a complementary direction, [arxiv_2109.12425] reformulates discrete architecture search as a continuous-action RL problem, enabling gradient-informed policy updates that partially bridge the conceptual divide between RL-based and differentiable NAS.

Scalability has also received dedicated attention. [arxiv_2410.01431] evaluates a novel RL-based agent on both NAS-Bench-101 and NAS-Bench-301 benchmarks, framing the objective not as finding a single optimal architecture but as learning a search strategy capable of consistently identifying strong candidates across varied settings. This reframing highlights an important conceptual shift: rather than treating NAS as a one-shot optimization, RL agents can be trained to generalize their search behavior. Overfitting within the search process itself is a related concern addressed by [arxiv_1905.02341], which identifies high-dimensional operator and skip-connection spaces as sources of instability in RL-based controllers and proposes refinement strategies to mitigate this. Together, these contributions illustrate that RL-based NAS remains an active and evolving area, with ongoing work addressing efficiency, scalability, and robustness in tandem.
