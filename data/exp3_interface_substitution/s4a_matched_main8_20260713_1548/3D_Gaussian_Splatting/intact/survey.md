# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Foundational Methods and Surveys of 3DGS

The landscape of novel-view synthesis was substantially reshaped by the introduction of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which addressed longstanding limitations of prior neural rendering approaches. The foundational method, presented by Kerbl et al. [arxiv_2308.04079], proposed representing scenes as collections of three-dimensional Gaussian ellipsoids rather than relying on implicit neural network encodings. This explicit, point-based representation enables real-time radiance field rendering at 1080p resolution for unbounded and complete scenes, a capability that prior methods struggled to achieve without sacrificing either speed or visual fidelity. By coupling the Gaussian scene representation with a differentiable, tile-based rasterization pipeline, the method achieves fast training times alongside high-quality novel-view synthesis, establishing it as a landmark contribution to the field.

The broader significance of 3DGS becomes clearer when situated against its primary predecessor, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). NeRF-based approaches represent scenes implicitly through position- and viewpoint-conditioned neural networks, queried via ray marching to produce volumetric renderings. While powerful, these methods are computationally expensive both to train and to render. As reviewed comprehensively by Gao et al. [arxiv_2210.00379], NeRF models found wide application across robotics, urban mapping, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality following their introduction in 2020; however, the same review acknowledges that Gaussian Splatting, emerging in August 2023, constitutes a direct competitor to the NeRF-based framework, marking a paradigm shift in how 3D scenes are represented and rendered.

The rapid adoption of 3DGS has prompted several survey efforts aimed at consolidating the growing body of downstream work. Wu et al. [arxiv_2403.11134] provide an early and comprehensive review of recent advances built upon the 3DGS foundation, noting that the method's use of Gaussian ellipsoids to model scenes enables efficient rendering that bypasses the neural network inference bottleneck characteristic of NeRF. Their survey catalogues extensions spanning dynamic scene modelling, scene editing, and integration with generative models, reflecting the breadth of research directions that the original method has catalysed.

Despite the practical success of 3DGS, its theoretical underpinnings have attracted scrutiny. Celarek et al. [arxiv_2502.19318] examine whether the rendering approximations inherent in 3DGS are in fact necessary for its strong performance. They observe that while NeRF is grounded in a principled ray-marching formulation for volumetric rendering, 3DGS employs a related but distinct and less rigorously justified rendering model. Their analysis investigates the consequences of this departure from accurate volumetric rendering, probing whether the empirical quality of 3DGS stems from the Gaussian representation itself or from specific properties of its approximate renderer. This line of inquiry complements the survey literature by moving beyond cataloguing applications to interrogating the foundational assumptions of the method, thereby providing a more complete theoretical picture of why and how 3DGS achieves its results. Together, these works — the original method [arxiv_2308.04079], its contextualisation within the NeRF lineage [arxiv_2210.00379], the survey of its extensions [arxiv_2403.11134], and the theoretical analysis of its rendering model [arxiv_2502.19318] — form the essential scholarly foundation for understanding 3D Gaussian Splatting.

### Compression, Efficiency, and Memory Optimization

The rapid adoption of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a real-time rendering paradigm has been accompanied by growing concern over its substantial memory and storage demands. While 3DGS offers clear advantages over Neural Radiance Fields in terms of rendering speed and training efficiency, the explicit representation of scenes through large collections of per-Gaussian parameters introduces significant overhead that limits deployment in resource-constrained environments.

A central challenge identified across the literature is that 3DGS requires storing numerous per-Gaussian attributes — including position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients for view-dependent appearance — resulting in memory footprints that scale poorly with scene complexity. As noted by [arxiv_2509.17083], 3DGS suffers from significant memory overhead due to its reliance on per-Gaussian parameters to model view-dependent effects and anisotropic shapes, a problem that has motivated a wave of compression and efficiency-focused research. Similarly, [arxiv_2312.04564] observes that while 3DGS achieves real-time rendering and accelerated training through rapid differentiable rasterization, the method demands substantial memory resources, making it difficult to deploy on hardware with limited capacity. This tension between representational fidelity and resource efficiency forms the core motivation for the works examined in this subsection.

One line of inquiry addresses efficiency through compact encoding of Gaussian attributes. [arxiv_2312.04564] introduces EAGLES, which proposes lightweight encodings to accelerate 3D Gaussian representations, targeting both memory consumption during training and storage requirements at inference time. By compressing the per-Gaussian parameter space, such approaches seek to retain the rendering quality of vanilla 3DGS while substantially reducing its resource footprint. This direction reflects a broader recognition that the raw parameterization of 3DGS, though effective, is far from parsimonious.

A complementary strategy involves restructuring the coordinate and feature representations themselves. [arxiv_2405.17083] presents F-3DGS, which factorizes both the spatial coordinates and appearance representations of 3D Gaussians, drawing inspiration from tensor decomposition methods previously applied to NeRF-based models. By decomposing the scene representation into lower-dimensional factors, F-3DGS reduces redundancy in the stored parameters and enables more efficient use of memory, while maintaining competitive rendering quality. This factorization approach is particularly relevant for deployment in real-time and resource-constrained settings, where the high computational costs of uncompressed representations are prohibitive [arxiv_2405.17083].

Hybrid approaches have also been explored as a means of balancing memory efficiency with rendering quality. [arxiv_2509.17083] proposes combining explicit Gaussian representations with complementary implicit components, arguing that neither paradigm alone achieves an optimal trade-off between memory consumption and visual fidelity. Such hybrid radiance field architectures attempt to offload the most memory-intensive aspects of 3DGS onto more compact implicit structures, thereby reducing overall overhead without sacrificing the real-time rendering capabilities that make 3DGS attractive.

Taken together, these works illustrate that memory optimization in 3DGS is being pursued through multiple, sometimes complementary strategies — lightweight encoding, factorized representations, and hybrid architectures — each targeting different sources of inefficiency in the original formulation.
