# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Foundational Methods and Surveys of 3DGS

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a paradigm for novel view synthesis represents one of the most significant developments in scene representation research in recent years. The foundational method was introduced by Kerbl et al. [arxiv_2308.04079], who proposed representing scenes as collections of 3D Gaussian ellipsoids that can be rendered in real time at 1080p resolution. Their approach addressed a critical limitation of prior methods: achieving high visual quality without incurring the prohibitive training and rendering costs associated with neural network-based representations. By leveraging differentiable rasterization of Gaussian primitives, the method achieved real-time performance for unbounded and complete scenes, a benchmark that no contemporary approach had previously met simultaneously with high visual fidelity [arxiv_2308.04079].

To situate 3DGS within the broader landscape of scene representation, it is instructive to contrast it with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), which had previously dominated the novel view synthesis literature. NeRF encodes a scene implicitly through position- and viewpoint-conditioned neural networks, relying on a principled ray-marching procedure for volumetric rendering [arxiv_2502.19318]. While NeRF models demonstrated impressive reconstruction quality and found applications across robotics, urban mapping, autonomous navigation, and augmented reality [arxiv_2210.00379], their computational demands during both training and inference remained a persistent bottleneck. In contrast, 3DGS replaces the implicit neural representation with an explicit set of Gaussian ellipsoids, enabling efficient rendering through splatting rather than ray marching [arxiv_2403.11134]. This architectural shift yields substantial gains in rendering speed while preserving competitive visual quality, positioning 3DGS as a direct and formidable competitor to the NeRF-based framework [arxiv_2210.00379].

An important theoretical question that has emerged in the wake of 3DGS's success concerns the fidelity of its rendering formulation. Celarek et al. [arxiv_2502.19318] examine whether 3DGS adheres to the principles of accurate volumetric rendering that underpin NeRF. They observe that, despite sharing a superficially similar rendering objective, 3DGS departs from the rigorous ray-marching approach of NeRF, raising questions about whether this approximation is a limitation or an acceptable trade-off given the method's empirical performance. This inquiry reflects a broader maturation of the field, in which researchers are moving beyond simply demonstrating that 3DGS works well in practice toward understanding why it does so.

Survey efforts have sought to consolidate the rapidly expanding body of work building upon the original 3DGS formulation. Wu et al. [arxiv_2403.11134] provide a comprehensive review of recent advances, cataloguing how the Gaussian splatting framework has been extended across diverse tasks and application domains. Their survey underscores that the acceleration in rendering speed afforded by 3DGS has catalyzed a wave of downstream research, with the explicit, editable nature of Gaussian primitives offering advantages over neural implicit representations in terms of interpretability and manipulability [arxiv_2403.11134]. Together, these foundational and survey contributions establish 3DGS as a central pillar of contemporary scene representation research, warranting careful examination of both its capabilities and its theoretical underpinnings.

### Compression, Efficiency, and Memory Optimization

The rapid adoption of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a real-time rendering paradigm has been accompanied by growing concern over its substantial memory and computational demands. While 3DGS offers clear advantages over Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) in terms of rendering speed and training efficiency [arxiv_2401.00834], the explicit, per-Gaussian parameterization that underpins its performance simultaneously introduces significant memory overhead, motivating a dedicated body of research into compression, efficiency, and memory optimization.

A central challenge is that 3DGS stores a large number of per-Gaussian attributes — including position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients for view-dependent appearance — resulting in high memory consumption during both training and inference. This burden is well documented across the literature: EAGLES [arxiv_2312.04564] notes that despite enabling real-time rendering and accelerated training through differentiable rasterization, 3DGS demands substantial memory resources for both storage and rendering. Similarly, HyRF [arxiv_2509.17083] identifies that the reliance on per-Gaussian parameters to model view-dependent effects and anisotropic shapes is the primary source of memory inefficiency in standard 3DGS pipelines.

In response, researchers have pursued several complementary strategies. One prominent direction involves factorizing or restructuring the Gaussian representation to reduce redundancy. F-3DGS [arxiv_2405.17083] proposes factorizing both the coordinates and the representations of 3D Gaussians, decoupling spatial positions from appearance attributes to achieve more compact scene descriptions. This factorization approach directly targets the high-dimensional per-Gaussian parameter space that drives memory costs, and it positions itself as a deployment-friendly alternative suitable for resource-constrained environments where standard 3DGS would be impractical [arxiv_2405.17083].

EAGLES [arxiv_2312.04564] takes a complementary approach by introducing lightweight encodings for Gaussian attributes, aiming to accelerate training and reduce memory footprints without sacrificing rendering quality. By compressing the internal representations of Gaussians rather than altering the scene structure itself, EAGLES addresses efficiency at the attribute level, offering a different trade-off compared to the structural factorization pursued by F-3DGS [arxiv_2405.17083].

HyRF [arxiv_2509.17083] adopts a hybrid strategy, combining explicit Gaussian representations with implicit components to achieve memory-efficient and high-quality novel view synthesis. This hybrid design acknowledges that purely explicit representations carry unavoidable per-primitive costs, and that selectively offloading certain aspects of scene modeling to implicit structures can yield meaningful memory savings. The authors explicitly situate their work within the broader trend of compressing 3DGS, suggesting that the field has converged on the recognition that unmodified 3DGS is not viable for many practical deployment scenarios [arxiv_2509.17083].

Taken together, these works illustrate a clear trajectory: as 3DGS has matured from a proof-of-concept into a widely used rendering framework, the community has increasingly prioritized making it leaner and more deployable. Whether through factorized coordinates [arxiv_2405.17083], lightweight encodings [arxiv_2312.04564], or hybrid representations [arxiv_2509.17083], the shared goal is to preserve the real-time rendering advantages of 3DGS while substantially reducing the memory costs that currently limit its applicability in resource-constrained and large-scale settings.
