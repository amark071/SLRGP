# 3D Gaussian Splatting

### Foundational Methods and Surveys of 3DGS

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a paradigm for novel view synthesis represents one of the most significant developments in scene representation research in recent years. The foundational method, introduced by Kerbl et al. [arxiv_2308.04079], addresses a fundamental tension in radiance field rendering: the trade-off between visual quality and computational efficiency. Prior to 3DGS, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) had established themselves as a powerful approach to novel view synthesis, yet their reliance on position- and viewpoint-conditioned neural networks imposed substantial training and inference costs [arxiv_2403.11134]. Kerbl et al. [arxiv_2308.04079] proposed representing scenes as collections of 3D Gaussian ellipsoids, enabling real-time rendering at 1080p resolution for unbounded and complete scenes — a capability that existing methods could not simultaneously achieve without sacrificing either speed or quality.

The contrast between 3DGS and NeRF-based approaches is a recurring theme in the literature. Where NeRF employs neural implicit representations that encode scene geometry and appearance within network weights, 3DGS adopts an explicit, point-based representation in which each Gaussian ellipsoid carries attributes such as position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonic coefficients for view-dependent appearance [arxiv_2403.11134]. This structural difference has direct consequences for rendering speed, as the splatting pipeline avoids the costly ray-marching procedures central to NeRF [arxiv_2210.00379]. Indeed, Gao et al. [arxiv_2210.00379] situate 3DGS as a direct competitor to the NeRF framework, noting its introduction in August 2023 as a pivotal moment that reshaped the landscape of implicit and explicit scene representation.

Despite its practical success, 3DGS raises important theoretical questions about the fidelity of its rendering formulation. Celarek et al. [arxiv_2502.19318] examine whether the approximate splatting-based rendering used in 3DGS, which departs from the principled volumetric ray-marching of NeRF, is actually necessary for achieving high visual quality. Their analysis highlights that while 3DGS shares a surface-level similarity with volumetric rendering, it does not strictly adhere to physically accurate volume integration. This observation invites scrutiny of the assumptions underlying the method's success and opens avenues for understanding when and why the approximation is sufficient.

Comprehensive surveys have sought to consolidate the rapid proliferation of work building upon the 3DGS foundation. Wu et al. [arxiv_2403.11134] provide a broad review of recent advances, cataloguing how 3DGS has been extended across domains including dynamic scene reconstruction, avatar generation, and large-scale scene modeling. Their survey underscores that the acceleration in rendering speed afforded by 3DGS has not only improved practical usability but has also greatly expanded the scope of applications that real-time radiance field methods can address. Together, these foundational contributions and surveys establish 3DGS as both a technically novel and practically transformative methodology, one whose explicit Gaussian representation offers complementary strengths to the neural implicit approaches that preceded it [arxiv_2210.00379], while simultaneously prompting ongoing investigation into the theoretical underpinnings of its rendering pipeline [arxiv_2502.19318].

### Compression, Efficiency, and Memory Optimization

The rapid adoption of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a real-time rendering paradigm has been accompanied by growing concern over its substantial memory and computational demands. While 3DGS offers clear advantages over Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) in terms of rendering speed and training efficiency [arxiv_2401.00834], the explicit representation of scenes through large collections of per-Gaussian parameters introduces significant overhead that limits deployment in resource-constrained environments.

A central challenge is that 3DGS requires storing numerous attributes per Gaussian — including position, covariance, opacity, and view-dependent colour encoded via spherical harmonics — resulting in memory footprints that scale poorly with scene complexity. As noted by Girish et al. [arxiv_2312.04564], 3DGS demands substantial memory resources for both training and rendering, motivating the development of lightweight encoding strategies. Their EAGLES framework addresses this by proposing efficient, accelerated representations that reduce memory consumption while preserving rendering fidelity, demonstrating that the quality–efficiency trade-off inherent to 3DGS is not insurmountable with careful architectural design.

Complementary efforts have explored factorisation as a means of reducing redundancy in Gaussian representations. Sun et al. [arxiv_2405.17083] introduce F-3DGS, which decouples Gaussian coordinates and their associated appearance representations into factorised components. By exploiting structural redundancies across the scene, this approach achieves more compact storage without sacrificing the rapid rendering speeds that make 3DGS attractive as an alternative to NeRF-like methods [arxiv_2405.17083]. This factorisation strategy reflects a broader trend in the field toward decomposing monolithic per-primitive representations into shared or low-rank components that are more amenable to compression.

The memory overhead of 3DGS is further compounded by its reliance on per-Gaussian parameters to model both view-dependent effects and anisotropic shapes, as highlighted by Wang and Xu [arxiv_2509.17083]. Their HyRF framework proposes a hybrid radiance field approach that combines the strengths of explicit Gaussian representations with more memory-efficient components, directly targeting the redundancy introduced by fine-grained per-primitive modelling. This hybrid philosophy — blending explicit and implicit representations — represents an emerging direction that seeks to retain the real-time rendering benefits of 3DGS while alleviating its memory burden [arxiv_2509.17083].

Taken together, these works reveal a consistent tension at the heart of 3DGS-based rendering: the explicit, optimisable nature of 3D Gaussians that enables real-time performance simultaneously gives rise to memory inefficiencies that hinder broader applicability. Approaches such as EAGLES [arxiv_2312.04564], F-3DGS [arxiv_2405.17083], and HyRF [arxiv_2509.17083] each address this tension through distinct strategies — lightweight encoding, factorised representations, and hybrid architectures, respectively — yet all share the common goal of making high-quality novel view synthesis viable in settings where memory and computational resources are limited. The convergence of these efforts underscores compression and efficiency as among the most active and consequential research directions within the 3DGS literature.
