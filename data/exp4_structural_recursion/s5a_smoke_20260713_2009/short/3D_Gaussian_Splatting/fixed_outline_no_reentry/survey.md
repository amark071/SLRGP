# 3D Gaussian Splatting (short)

### Foundational Methods and Surveys of 3DGS

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) represents a pivotal shift in the landscape of novel view synthesis and 3D scene representation. Prior to its introduction, Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) had dominated the field since 2020, offering a principled, implicit neural network-based approach to scene representation that found applications across robotics, urban mapping, autonomous navigation, and virtual reality [arxiv_2210.00379]. While NeRF-based methods demonstrated impressive visual fidelity, they relied on computationally expensive ray-marching procedures and volumetric rendering pipelines that imposed significant training and inference costs, making real-time rendering at high resolutions effectively unattainable [arxiv_2308.04079].

The seminal work introducing 3DGS addressed these limitations directly by proposing a fundamentally different representational paradigm [arxiv_2308.04079]. Rather than encoding scene geometry and appearance implicitly within the weights of a neural network, 3DGS models a scene as a collection of 3D Gaussian ellipsoids, each parameterized by position, covariance, opacity, and view-dependent color. These primitives are initialized from sparse point clouds produced during camera calibration and subsequently optimized through a differentiable rasterization pipeline. The key innovation lies in the rendering strategy: Gaussian ellipsoids are projected and rasterized into image space, enabling high-quality novel view synthesis at real-time frame rates exceeding 30 frames per second at 1080p resolution—a benchmark that no prior method had achieved for unbounded, complete scenes [arxiv_2308.04079]. This combination of competitive training times and state-of-the-art visual quality established 3DGS as an immediate reference method in the field.

A critical distinction between 3DGS and its NeRF predecessors lies in the nature of their rendering formulations. NeRF employs a theoretically grounded volumetric rendering integral evaluated through ray marching, whereas 3DGS adopts a hybrid solution that blends principles of volume rendering with the efficiency of primitive rasterization [arxiv_2502.19318]. This hybrid approach introduces a set of deliberate approximations relative to strict volumetric rendering theory, and a natural question arises as to whether these approximations constitute a meaningful sacrifice in representational accuracy. Recent work has examined this question in depth, analyzing whether replacing 3DGS's approximations with more principled volumetric rendering solutions yields measurable quality improvements [arxiv_2502.19318]. Such investigations underscore that the performance advantages of 3DGS are not incidental but are architecturally embedded in its rendering design choices.

Beyond rendering speed, the explicit nature of the Gaussian representation confers additional practical advantages that implicit neural representations struggle to match. Because scene content is encoded in discrete, interpretable geometric primitives rather than distributed across network parameters, 3DGS naturally facilitates downstream editing operations. Tasks such as dynamic scene reconstruction, geometry editing, and physical simulation become more tractable when the underlying representation is explicit and spatially localized [arxiv_2403.11134]. This has catalyzed a rapid proliferation of derivative works extending 3DGS to a broad range of applications, including 3D reconstruction, scene editing, and various specialized downstream tasks [arxiv_2403.11134].

The pace of development in this area has been remarkable. Following the August 2023 introduction of 3DGS, research interest shifted substantially away from NeRF-centric frameworks, with Gaussian Splatting overtaking NeRF as the dominant paradigm for novel view synthesis in terms of community attention and publication volume [arxiv_2210.00379]. Comprehensive surveys have since emerged to organize and contextualize this growing body of literature [arxiv_2403.11134], reflecting both the maturity and the continued dynamism of the field. Collectively, these developments position 3DGS not merely as an incremental improvement over NeRF but as a structurally distinct approach that redefines the trade-off space between rendering quality, computational efficiency, and scene editability.

### Compression, Efficiency, and Memory Optimization

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as a scene representation paradigm has prompted a substantial body of research addressing its core limitations, particularly those concerning memory efficiency, storage overhead, and robustness to imperfect input data. While 3DGS offers compelling advantages over Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) approaches in terms of real-time rendering and training speed, the explicit per-Gaussian parameterization that underpins these advantages simultaneously introduces significant resource demands that constrain practical deployment.

A central challenge identified across multiple works is the memory and storage cost inherent to representing scenes with millions of individual Gaussians, each carrying its own set of parameters. EAGLES [arxiv_2312.04564] directly confronts this problem by introducing quantized embeddings to reduce per-point memory storage requirements, complemented by a coarse-to-fine training strategy designed to stabilize and accelerate optimization. Crucially, EAGLES also incorporates a pruning stage that yields scene representations with fewer Gaussians overall, thereby improving both training and rendering throughput without sacrificing visual fidelity. This approach treats compression primarily as a quantization and sparsification problem, operating directly on the Gaussian point cloud rather than introducing auxiliary representational structures.

F-3DGS [arxiv_2405.17083] takes a complementary but conceptually distinct route, drawing inspiration from classical matrix and tensor factorization techniques. Rather than pruning or quantizing individual Gaussians, F-3DGS factorizes the coordinates and representations of dense Gaussian clusters, approximating them in a compact factorized form. This strategy drastically reduces storage requirements while aiming to preserve image quality, and it reflects a broader trend in the field of applying well-established linear algebra decompositions to the novel challenge of Gaussian-based scene compression.

HyRF [arxiv_2509.17083] offers a more architecturally integrated solution by proposing a hybrid representation that combines explicit Gaussians with grid-based neural fields. The authors argue that purely explicit representations suffer from memory overhead due to per-Gaussian modeling of view-dependent effects and anisotropic shapes, while prior neural compression approaches struggle to capture high-frequency spatial variations in Gaussian properties, leading to degraded fine-detail reconstruction. HyRF addresses this tension by decomposing the scene into a compact set of explicit Gaussians that retain only critical high-frequency parameters, while delegating the prediction of remaining properties to the neural field component. This hybrid design can be seen as a synthesis of the strengths identified in both the explicit 3DGS paradigm and the implicit neural field tradition, and it stands in contrast to the more purely explicit compression strategies of EAGLES and F-3DGS.

Beyond memory and storage, a separate but equally important line of work addresses the degradation of 3DGS rendering quality when training images are corrupted by blur. Deblurring 3D Gaussian Splatting [arxiv_2401.00834] identifies that while 3DGS achieves remarkable visual quality under clean input conditions, it suffers severe degradation when training images exhibit blur arising from lens defocus, object motion, or camera shake. The authors note that prior attempts to address blur in neural rendering have predominantly relied on volumetric neural networks, which are costly and incompatible with the real-time rendering goals of 3DGS. Their work thus extends the 3DGS framework to handle blurry inputs, situating itself at the intersection of robust reconstruction and efficient rendering.

Taken together, these works illustrate that the 3DGS literature is simultaneously pursuing two broad directions: reducing the resource footprint of the representation through compression, quantization, factorization, and hybridization [arxiv_2312.04564, arxiv_2405.17083, arxiv_2509.17083], and extending the robustness of the framework to challenging real-world acquisition conditions [arxiv_2401.00834]. Both directions reflect the community's effort to translate the theoretical promise of 3DGS into practical, deployable systems.
